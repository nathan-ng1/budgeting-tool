Using the Dashboard

A tour of the Dashboard's five tabs, and what each of the scripts in the repo root actually does — for day-to-day use once the Installation Pack has already set things up.

New here? This guide assumes setup is already done. If you haven't installed yet, see docs/setup-guide.html (Windows) or docs/setup-guide-mac.html (macOS) instead.
Windows vs. Mac: every script below lives in windows/ on Windows and mac/ on macOS, with a matching name (.bat vs. .command) — e.g. Windows' windows\open_dashboard.bat is macOS's mac/open_dashboard.command. The tables use the shared base name only.

How to use?

  1. Download a new Statement Export into your Transactions Inbox, then run process_statement_export.bat — or, on the Dashboard-only path, add anything new by hand on the Transactions tab.
  2. Double-click open_dashboard.bat and check the Overview tab for the current month.
  3. Adjust category budgets on the Budget tab if anything's drifted, or run generate_budget_suggestion.bat for a fresh AI write-up.
  4. Saving toward something specific? Add a Goal on the Goals tab, then log each amount you put aside with Add contribution — or divide a month's leftover money across several Goals at once with Split savings.
  5. Add or edit a Recurring rule on Settings whenever a standing item changes (a subscription price rise, a new one starting).

The Dashboard Tabs

Overview

Where your money went this month — or this year

Pick a month with the Jul–Jun pills, or Full year for the whole Financial Year. Shows the Income/Expenses/Bills & Subscriptions/Debt/Available to Save/Saved stat tiles, Income Allocation with its Balance check card, then a Goals section with the Saving Goals tile, then a Breakdown section that shows one topic at a time: pick Budgeted vs Actual, Spending, Debt or Bills & Subscriptions, and Full year also offers Month by month. Month by month holds Cash Flow by Month and Month by Month. Spending holds Spending by Category and Top expenses, then the Outflows breakdown — in Full year, a full-width chart of stacked columns, one per month, split into Expenses, Bills & Subscriptions and Debt, each segment showing its share of that month's Outflows (hover for the amount; the legend gives each Type's share of the year to date, and months not yet reached are empty); in a month, Spending over time and Movers follow it. The section you pick stays picked as you move between months. Top expenses lists your largest single Spending Transactions (the top 5 for a month, the top 10 for Full year), each with its Notes, Category, date and amount. Debt and Beem Adjustment are left out. Bills & Subscriptions lists every Bills & Subscriptions merchant over the trailing 12 months ending at the selected month (or the last elapsed month in Full year): its Category, how many charges, when it was last charged, the typical charge (the median) and its cadence (Weekly, Fortnightly, Monthly, Quarterly or Yearly when the typical gap between charges is close to one of those, otherwise Every N days; or N charges when they all fell on one date). Each row projects a yearly cost — typical charge × the usual number of charges a year for a named cadence (52, 26, 12, 4 or 1), or × 365 ÷ days between charges otherwise, so a fortnightly gym fee shows its full-year weight — and a one-off shows what you actually paid. Rows run biggest yearly cost first, and the header totals the cost per year and per month. It has its own section in the Breakdown, so it is not confused with the Recurring Transactions in Settings. Movers (single month only, in Spending beside Spending over time) shows which Spending Categories rose or fell most. Pick what to compare against from the dropdown — Previous month, Avg of preceding 3 months (the default) or Avg of preceding 12 months, months with no spending counting as zero — and the card switches at once. Each Category has a bar growing from a centre line (right for a rise, left for a fall, scaled to the biggest change) with its Difference ($) and Difference (%); a line under the headings names the comparison. New means the comparison was nothing, and a dash means no change. Biggest change comes first, and it states the facts without advice. Spending is Expenses plus Bills & Subscriptions: Spending over time and Spending by Category both include your Bills & Subscriptions Categories, Debt and Savings stay out, and Beem Adjustment reimbursements still reduce the figures. The Spending by Category total equals the Spending over time total for the same month. Available to Save is Income minus Expenses, Bills & Subscriptions and Debt — Savings isn't subtracted, so it's how much you could have put into Savings. Unallocated subtracts Savings too — did every dollar get a job? It reads Balanced at exactly $0 and Over income when negative. The Saving Goals tile, in the Goals section under Income Allocation, shows up to your three current Goals (every Goal not yet in Past goals, so Achieved, Ended and Upcoming ones included), soonest end date first: each with its status, a progress bar with a tick for where an even pace would have you today, how much of the target is funded and how much to save per month (or, for an Achieved or Ended Goal, how it finished). The header counts your current Goals and View all goals jumps to the Goals tab; with none it says there are no active goals yet. Unlike the rest of the Overview it ignores the month pills and the Financial Year switcher, since a Goal's figures are all-time. Read-only — nothing here is edited directly.

Transactions

The full list, searchable and editable

Add transaction enters one by hand — this is how a Dashboard-only install (no AI subscription) records spending, instead of running process_statement_export.bat. Every row can be edited or deleted. Search by Notes, sort any column, and export/import via the "More options" menu.

Choose the Goals Category (under Savings) and a required Goal picker appears, listing your Active Goals — the row becomes a Contribution to that Goal, with its Notes pre-filled with the Goal's title when you add it (editing never rewrites Notes). With no Active Goals, Goals stays in the Category dropdown but greyed out, labelled "(no active Goals)". Goals rows show the Goal's current title as a chip beside the Category, muted and tagged "(archived)" or "(deleted)" once the Goal has moved to Past goals. Edit a Savings or Investments row to Category Goals plus a Goal to turn it into a Contribution, or move it back to clear the link; changing the Goal while staying on Goals moves the Contribution. This is also where you edit or delete an individual Contribution — the Goals tab's Details view is read-only. Goals rows export, but Import refuses them.

Budget

Per-category monthly budgets, plus the AI write-up

Set a budget amount per Category per month in the grid — this is what Overview's Budgeted vs Actual compares against. The Budget Suggestion card shows the standing write-up from generate_budget_suggestion.bat, editable in place. That script needs an AI subscription configured first (see the table below) — until then, the card just explains how to set one up.

Goals

Savings goals with a monthly target

Add a Goal with New goal: a title, an optional description, a target amount, a start date (today by default; it can be in the past or future) and the date you want to reach it by. Titles are unique across all your Goals, including Past goals, ignoring case. The header counts your current Goals, whatever their status chip; archived and deleted ones are listed under Past goals instead. Goals follow no Financial Year, so this tab has no year switcher, and a Goal's figures count every Contribution to it whatever its date.

Each Goal's card shows a status chip, how much to save per month to finish on time (with the months left), a bar with a tick marking where an even pace would have you today, the amount saved of the target, and an Original plan hint — what the target would have needed per month from the start date. The chip reads On track when you're at or ahead of an even pace and Behind when you're not; Achieved once saved reaches the target (overshooting is fine), Ended when the end date passes with the target unmet (edit the end date to extend it), and Upcoming before the start date. Months count the current month and the end month, with a minimum of one.

Add contribution on a card takes an amount, a date and Notes (the Goal's title by default) and records a Savings Transaction in the locked Goals Category, linked to that Goal. Edit changes the Goal's fields; Archive and Delete both move it to Past goals without touching its Contributions — there is no permanent delete. Details opens a read-only view of one Goal: its needs-per-month, saved, left, remaining time, dates and original plan, a trajectory chart of your saved total against the target and an even pace, and a ledger of every Contribution. Use ← All goals to go back.

Split savings divides one amount across your Active Goals by percentage. The total starts from the Unallocated figure of the month selected on Overview (this month, if Full year is selected) when it is positive, and you can change it; the percentages start from your last split, remembered in this browser. They can't add up to more than 100%, and whatever is left over is written as one Transaction in Savings or Investments, your choice (none if the Goals take exactly 100%). A preview lists every Transaction before you press Write split, and they are all written together or not at all.

The collapsed Past goals list at the bottom holds archived and deleted Goals, each with an Archived or Deleted badge and its saved amount. Reactivate opens the Goal's edit form — handy for extending the end date — and saving it puts the Goal back among your active ones.

Settings

Categories and Recurring Transactions

Category Management adds, renames, or deletes the Categories offered everywhere else in the Dashboard (a locked Category, like Beem Adjustment or Goals, can't be renamed or deleted; Goals is managed by the Goals tab, though you can still set a monthly Budget for it). Recurring Transactions adds, edits, or deletes standing rules (salary, rent, subscriptions) — each rule's Day follows its Start Date, and expands automatically alongside any Statement Export you process. Goals isn't offered as a rule's Category, since every Contribution has to belong to a Goal.

Opening and closing the Dashboard

Everything runs locally — the Dashboard is a small web server on your own machine, reading your local database directly. No transaction data ever leaves it (ADR-0008).

ScriptWhat it does
open_dashboard Double-click to start the server and open the Dashboard in Chrome (falling back to your default browser). If it's already running, this just opens the page instead of starting a second server. Leave its window open while you use the Dashboard. Also prints a one-line "update available" notice here, at most once a day, if a newer Release exists — see update below.
close_dashboard Stops whatever Dashboard server is currently running. Use this after pulling code changes so the next open_dashboard actually picks up the new code, rather than silently still serving the old one.

The other scripts, in windows/ or mac/

ScriptWhen to run it
process_statement_export Whenever you've downloaded a new Statement Export into your Transactions Inbox. Sanitises it, categorises every transaction via your configured AI backend (pausing for Needs Review items in the terminal), then writes it to the Transaction Log. Pass --dry-run to preview without writing. Full walkthrough: docs/agents/statement-export-pipeline.md.
generate_budget_suggestion Regenerates the standing Budget Suggestion write-up shown on the Budget tab, from recent spending history. Replaces whatever write-up was there before — one standing document, not a history. Refresh the Dashboard's Budget tab afterwards to see it. Requires an AI subscription (Claude Code or Codex CLI) — setup configures this automatically (as ADVISOR_BACKEND in .env) when you answer yes to the AI subscription prompt. Not available on the Dashboard-only path; re-run setup to add it later.
setup The guided installer. Safe to re-run any time — e.g. to add the AI-categorisation path to a Dashboard-only install. See docs/setup-guide.html / docs/setup-guide-mac.html.
update Pulls the latest Release and re-syncs dependencies, after showing you what's changed and asking you to confirm. Run this when open_dashboard's notice says an update is available.

tests/dev/windows/ and tests/dev/mac/ hold a separate open_dashboard_dev/close_dashboard_dev pair, for people working on the frontend's source code itself (hot-reloading Vite dev server rather than the built static page) — not needed for normal day-to-day use.
